1.1. Project description — facts to write into ≥4 sentences (your words)

  • Single-axis reaction wheel attitude-control demonstrator for a CubeSat-style satellite.
  • A ~1U (10 cm cube, ≤1.33 kg) body rotates about a vertical axis on a low-friction bearing mounted on a fixed base clamped to the bench.
  • A BLDC motor spins a metal flywheel (≤6000 rpm firmware limit) to rotate the body; controlled by an ESP32-S3 module on a custom PCB with a field-oriented-control motor driver and a MEMS gyro.
  • Powered on the rig by an onboard protected 3S Li-ion pack (BMS + fuse), placed to balance the body. New boards are first brought up on a current-limited 12 V bench PSU.
  • Commands and telemetry between the ESP32 and a laptop over its built-in wireless (Bluetooth LE or Wi-Fi); motor disabled automatically if the link drops. Physical power-isolation switch on the body.
  • Work: PCB assembly (hand soldering, SMD pick-and-place after training), bench testing, operating the rig. PCB fabrication, laser cutting and 3D printing are done by technicians via fabrication requests.

2. Hazards and controls

L = likelihood, C = consequence, R = L × C, all scored with the controls listed in place. Bands: 1–4 acceptable, 5–9 adequate, 10–16 tolerable (stop), 17–25 unacceptable.

No. Hazard Harm / who Controls L C R Add’l?
1 Flywheel burst / fragment ejection Eye or penetrating injury to user and bystanders from parts released at speed (wheel stores up to ~20 J at max speed) Wheel machined from aluminium/brass — never 3D-printed or glued. Speed hard-limited in firmware (≤6000 rpm) and below motor rating. Wheel fully enclosed in a polycarbonate (or metal) shroud before any spin above a low test speed; acrylic not used as a guard. Wheel balanced and fasteners thread-locked; inspected for cracks/looseness before each session. Safety glasses during spin tests. Rig kept to the back of the bench, nobody in line with the wheel plane. 1 4 4 N
2 Entanglement / contact with rotating parts Hair, sleeves, fingers caught by wheel or rotating body; cuts, bruising Shroud over wheel. Long hair tied back, no loose sleeves/lanyards. Hands clear of rotating body whenever the system is armed. Wireless stop ready before arming (see #3). 2 2 4 N
3 Unexpected start-up / runaway Firmware fault or unstable controller drives wheel to max speed or whips the body; impact injury, damage, knocks items off bench Three independent stops, all in place before the first battery-powered spin: (1) stop command from the laptop (single key/button in the control software); (2) firmware watchdog disables the motor driver if the laptop’s heartbeat is not received for >0.5 s; (3) latching power-isolation switch on the top face of the body. On the bench PSU, the PSU output switch is the stop. Firmware arming sequence + speed and current limits. Current limited by PSU setting or battery fuse/BMS. New firmware/gains first tested with the body clamped or the wheel speed limit reduced. Note: wheel coasts after power removal — keep hands clear until stopped. 2 3 6 N
4 Mains electric shock (bench PSU, oscilloscope, soldering station) Electrocution of user Department-supplied, PAT-tested equipment only; inspect leads before use; no modification of mains equipment; no extension daisy-chains; nothing project-built connected to mains (project circuits are 12 V DC). 1 5 5 N
5 Low-voltage high-current faults (short circuit, wrong polarity, regenerative over-voltage, capacitor failure) Burns from hot components/wires, capacitor venting, small fire PSU current limit set (≤3 A; lower at first power-up); battery fused at the pack. Input fuse and reverse-polarity protection on PCB. Brake resistor/TVS + bulk capacitance for regenerative energy; capacitors rated ≥25 V on the 12 V rail. First power-up of each new board at low current limit with visual/thermal check. Never leave powered rig unattended. 2 3 6 N
6 Hot surfaces (motor, driver ICs, brake resistor) Minor burns Allow cool-down; touch-test only with back of finger after power-off; brake resistor mounted away from handling points. 2 2 4 N
7 Soldering — burns Burns to user Iron returned to stand every time; not left unattended while on or cooling; work area clear. 2 2 4 N
8 Soldering — fumes Occupational asthma (flux fumes) Lead-free solder; fume extractor on for all soldering; board elevated/angled to keep face out of plume; limit session length. 1 3 3 N
9 Soldering — fire Fire involving flammables near iron Flammables (including IPA) kept away; iron switched off at end of session; never placed on flammable surfaces. 1 5 5 N
10 SMD assembly (solder paste, pick-and-place, reflow) Skin contact/ingestion of paste (metal + flux); burns from reflow oven; fumes Only after Canvas-booked training. Nitrile gloves when handling paste; wash hands; no food/drink. Reflow oven used per training with extraction; boards cooled before handling. 1 3 3 N
11 Isopropyl alcohol (flux cleaning) Flammable; eye/skin irritation, vapour Small dispensing bottle only; away from iron and hot boards; ventilated area; safety glasses; follow SDS/COSHH. 1 3 3 N
12 Hand tools / cutting component leads Cuts; flying lead offcuts into eyes Safety glasses when trimming leads/cutting; hold offcut while cutting; correct tool for job; read EEE hand-tool SOP. 1 3 3 N
13 Lone / out-of-hours working Delayed help after any of the above Hardware work only during lab opening hours (9–5) with others present; no rig testing alone. 1 4 4 N
14 Fatigue / rushing near deadlines Increased error rate in all tasks above Plan to avoid last-minute build; breaks during long sessions; no powered testing when tired. 2 2 4 N
15 Li-ion battery fire / thermal runaway (short circuit, over-discharge, mechanical damage, defective cells) Fire, toxic smoke, burns to user and others in the lab Protected pack with BMS (over-charge, over-discharge, over-current, short-circuit) from a reputable supplier, bought via the department — no unbranded cells. Fuse (≤5 A) at the pack terminals. Keyed, polarised connector (e.g. XT30); no exposed terminals. Pack held in a rigid mount outside the wheel plane, so wheel fragments cannot reach it, and away from sharp edges. Firmware low-voltage cut-off as backup to the BMS. Inspected for swelling, damage or heat before each use; damaged packs taken out of service and disposed of via technicians. Rig never left powered unattended. Know the extinguisher location and evacuation route. 1 5 5 N
16 Li-ion charging and storage Fire from overcharging or a faulty charger, during charging or in storage Department-approved charger (UKCA/CE marked) matched to chemistry and cell count. Charged in the lab only, during working hours, attended, with the pack removed from the rig, on a non-flammable surface inside a fire-resistant battery bag; not charged overnight. Charging stopped if the pack becomes hot or swollen. Stored in a fire-resistant bag at storage charge, as directed by the technicians; the battery is not taken home. 1 5 5 N
